A typesetting system takes a description of a document and decides where every character goes. It breaks paragraphs into lines and stacks the lines into pages. The command \verb|\section{Name}| starts a section, and \verb+a  b+ keeps spaces.

\begin{verbatim}
def f (x : Nat) :=
  x + 1   -- two spaces
\end{verbatim}

Lines of a paragraph are chosen together rather than one at a time, so that the spacing is as even as the words allow and few hyphens appear.

\begin{verbatim}
one
    two
\end{verbatim}
A last paragraph with \verb!x_1^2! inline.
